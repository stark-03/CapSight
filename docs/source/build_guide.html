How the finished system works

The cap camera does no thinking. It only streams video. All the AI runs on the phone, and the only output is speech.

What the user hears

SituationSpoken instruction
Something within 1.2 m straight ahead“Stop. Chair ahead.”
Stairs within 4 m“Caution. Stairs ahead.”
Something within 2.5 m, one side is clear“Person ahead. Move left.”
All three directions blocked“Path blocked. Stop and turn.”
Nothing nearby“Path clear.” (at most every 6 s)
Camera switched off or out of range“Camera disconnected. Please stop.”

Check twice before powering on. Red to 5V and black to GND. Reversing them, or putting 5 V on the 3V3 pin, destroys the board. Measure with the multimeter first: you should read about 5.0 V between the 5V and GND pins. Do not insert an SD card; IO14 and IO15 are shared with the SD slot.

Build steps

1

Collect and check the parts

  • Every BUY item from the shopping list has arrived.
  • ESP32-CAM: the camera ribbon is fully pushed into its connector and the latch is closed.
  • Power bank charged to full.
  • Arduino IDE 2.x installed with the “esp32 by Espressif Systems” board package.
2

Flash the camera firmware

  1. Press the ESP32-CAM onto the MB programmer, camera facing up, pins lined up. Connect it to the laptop with a micro-USB data cable (charge-only cables don’t work).
  2. Open esp32_cam_ap/esp32_cam_ap.ino in Arduino IDE. Choose Board: AI Thinker ESP32-CAM, Partition: Huge APP (3MB No OTA), Upload speed: 115200, and the CH340 COM port.
  3. Click Upload. If you see “Failed to connect”, hold the IO0 button on the MB, tap RST, release IO0, and upload again.
  4. After “Done uploading”, tap RST once. Open Serial Monitor at 115200; it should print that the hotspot BLENAV_CAM started.
3

Test the video on your phone

  1. Keep the board on the MB, powered by USB.
  2. On the phone, join Wi-Fi BLENAV_CAM, password blenav1234.
  3. Open http://192.168.4.1 in Chrome, then http://192.168.4.1:81/stream. You should see live video.
  4. Wave a hand in front of the lens: the delay should be well under a second.
  5. If the picture is upside down or very pink, note it; the OV3660 fix in the firmware handles this, so report it if it still happens.
4

Make the power connection

  1. Remove the ESP32-CAM from the MB programmer. From now on the MB stays on the desk.
  2. Solder the 1000 µF capacitor to the top-side solder joints of the 5V and GND pins (left column, first two pins). Long leg (+) to 5V. Striped side (−) to GND. Keep the legs short.
  3. Push the power cable’s DuPont ends onto the pins: red → 5V, black → GND.
  4. Plug the USB end into the power bank. Check with the multimeter: about 5.0 V between 5V and GND.
  5. Leave it streaming to the phone for 30 minutes. Pass if the video never drops and the metal shield is warm but not too hot to touch.
If the stream keeps dropping, the board is browning out. Use a shorter or thicker cable, check the capacitor polarity, and try another power bank. Some power banks switch off when the load is very small; the camera draws enough (about 0.2–0.3 A) that this is rare.
5

Connect the tilt sensor (MPU6050)

  1. Solder the header pins onto the MPU6050 if they came loose.
  2. Connect four jumper wires exactly as in the wiring diagram: 3V3→VCC, GND→GND, IO14→SCL, IO15→SDA.
  3. The tilt-reading code is a firmware addition still to be written (SRS requirement FR-CAM-05). Until then the sensor can stay connected and unused; the camera works without it.
6

Build the camera box

  1. Cut a ~14 mm lens hole in the front of the box. Cut 3–4 air slots in the top. Drill a small hole at the back for the cable.
  2. Fix the ESP32-CAM upright behind the lens hole with foam tape so the lens looks straight out. Add the heatsink on the metal shield if you bought one.
  3. Stick the MPU6050 flat on the bottom of the box with foam tape. It must not move relative to the camera, or the tilt reading will be wrong.
  4. Secure the cable inside with a dab of hot glue so a tug on the cable can’t pull the pins off.
7

Mount the box on the cap

  1. Stick one Velcro half on the box and the other on the front panel of the cap, just above the peak, centred.
  2. Glue a small foam wedge behind the box so the lens points 25–30° downward when the user’s head is level.
  3. Run the cable over the top of the cap to the back strap, then down the collar to the back pocket. Fix it every 10–15 cm with cable clips. Leave a little slack at the neck so turning the head doesn’t pull.
  4. Put the power bank in the back pocket. Check: total weight on the cap under 100 g.
8

Set up the phone

  1. Pair the Bluetooth earphones (Settings → Bluetooth). Play any audio to confirm sound comes through them.
  2. Join BLENAV_CAM. When Android says the network has no internet, tap Yes / Stay connected. Turn off “Switch to mobile data” / “Adaptive Wi-Fi” so the phone doesn’t drop the camera.
  3. Install the navigation app and enter the user’s height and the camera field of view (66° for the standard lens).
9

10

Test on a marked course

  • AT-01: walking at the chair → “Chair ahead. Move …” at about 2.5 m, “Stop” at about 1.2 m.
  • AT-02: chair on the left → “Move right”.
  • AT-03: person crosses → warning before 1.2 m, never “Path clear” while they are in the way.
  • AT-06: switch off the power bank mid-walk → “Camera disconnected. Please stop.” within 2 s.
  • AT-09: objects at 1, 2, 3, 4 m → distance within ±20%.
  • AT-10: a blindfolded teammate walks the course with the cane and the system, no collisions.
  • Record every run on video; keep the best one as the demo fallback.
11

Demo-day checklist

  • Power bank and phone fully charged; spare power bank in the bag.
  • Cap unit tested in the actual demo room lighting.
  • Earphones paired and charged.
  • Phone: Wi-Fi “stay connected” set, mobile data switching off.
  • Debug view open on a second screen so evaluators can see boxes and distances.
  • Recorded fallback video ready.
  • White cane in hand during the demo.
  • SRS, Phase-2 report and this guide printed.
