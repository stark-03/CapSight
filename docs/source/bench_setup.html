CapSight · Phase B bench test · no battery

Run CapSight with the ESP32-CAM on the MB, powered from the laptop

The ESP32-CAM sits on its ESP32-CAM-MB programmer, and one micro-USB cable from the laptop powers it. The board makes its own Wi-Fi hotspot. The phone joins that hotspot, runs the detection and speaks the warnings. The laptop only supplies power and shows the board's log. It does not need to join the camera's Wi-Fi.

Wi-Fi name
BLENAV_CAM
Password
blenav1234
Camera address
http://192.168.4.1
Serial baud
115200
Expected video
320×240 · ~11 fps

Steps

  1. Assemble the stack and plug it into the laptop

    • Lift the camera connector latch on the ESP32-CAM, push the OV3660 ribbon fully in, close the latch.
    • Clip the ESP32-CAM onto the ESP32-CAM-MB. All pins go in, and the MB's micro-USB port faces away from the camera.
    • Connect the MB to a USB port on the laptop itself with a short data cable. Avoid hubs and long thin cables: the board draws up to about 500 mA while streaming.
  2. first time, or after a firmware change

    Flash the firmware from Arduino IDE

    Open firmware/esp32_cam_ap/esp32_cam_ap.ino and set:

    Board        AI Thinker ESP32-CAM
Partition    Huge APP (3MB No OTA)
Upload speed 115200
Port         the MB's port (e.g. /dev/ttyUSB0 or /dev/ttyACM0)

    Click Upload. The MB switches the board into flash mode by itself. If you see Failed to connect to ESP32, hold IO0 on the MB, tap RST on the ESP32-CAM, release IO0, then click Upload again. After the upload finishes, tap RST once so the new firmware starts.

  3. Check that the board started

    In Arduino IDE open Tools → Serial Monitor at 115200 baud, then tap RST.

    You should see: the BLENAV_CAM banner with Join WiFi : BLENAV_CAM and the stream address. [cam] INIT FAILED means the ribbon is not seated: unplug the cable, reseat the ribbon, plug it back in.
  4. optional

    Check the stream from the laptop

    bash tools/stream_check.sh

    It moves the laptop onto BLENAV_CAM for about 30 seconds, records 10 s of video, saves the first frame to /tmp/capsight_stream_check/first_frame.jpg, then switches back to your normal Wi-Fi. The camera serves one viewer at a time, so close any browser tab showing 192.168.4.1 before the phone connects.

  5. Join the phone to the camera's Wi-Fi

    Phone Settings → Wi-Fi → BLENAV_CAM → password blenav1234. When Android says the network has no internet, choose Stay connected (or "Keep Wi-Fi connection"). Mobile data can stay on, because the app sends its camera traffic over this Wi-Fi network only.

  6. first time, or after an app change

    Install the app on the phone

    With a second USB cable to the phone (USB debugging on), from the project folder:

    export JAVA_HOME=/var/lib/flatpak/app/com.google.AndroidStudio/current/active/files/extra/jbr
./gradlew --no-daemon installDebug

    Or send app/build/outputs/apk/debug/app-debug.apk over WhatsApp as a document and tap it. Use the USB cable, not wireless debugging, because the phone leaves your home Wi-Fi when it joins BLENAV_CAM.

  7. Point the app at the real camera and start

    • Open CapSight → Debug view → Frame source: ESP32-CAM over Wi-Fi.
    • Tap Use real cap (192.168.4.1) and leave Bind to Wi-Fi network on.
    • Go back and tap the big green Start navigation button.
    You should hear "Navigation started". The debug view shows Camera connected and cam ~11 fps 320x240, with FOV 54° (cap setting).

  8. Try it

    • Aim the camera at a chair. At about 2.5 m you should hear "Chair ahead. Move left/right." At about 1.2 m you should hear "Stop. Chair ahead." and feel a vibration.
    • Pull the USB cable out of the MB. Within about 2 s the phone says "Camera disconnected. Please stop." Plug it back in: the board reboots in a few seconds and the phone says "Camera connected." by itself (allow up to about 10 s).
    • To finish, tap Stop navigation or use Stop in the notification. You should hear "Navigation stopped".

If something goes wrong

What you seeLikely causeFix
Board reboots every few seconds, stream keeps droppingBrownout: the USB port or cable cannot supply enough currentUse a shorter, thicker cable directly on a laptop port, not a hub
BLENAV_CAM missing from the phone's Wi-Fi listFirmware not running, or still in flash modeTap RST and check the Serial Monitor for the banner
Upload fails with "Failed to connect to ESP32"Board did not enter flash modeHold IO0, tap RST, release IO0, upload again
App stays on "Connecting to camera"Phone not on BLENAV_CAM, address changed, or Bind to Wi-Fi offRejoin BLENAV_CAM; in Debug view tap "Use real cap" and turn Bind on
Connected but 0 fps, or a frozen pictureCamera clock raised above 8 MHz (it jams this board's antenna), or a browser tab is holding the streamClose the browser tab; reset the clock with http://192.168.4.1/control?xclk=8 or tap RST
Phone drops BLENAV_CAM after a minuteAndroid prefers networks with internetForget and rejoin, choose "Stay connected"; turn off "Switch to mobile data" if offered

This is the bench setup for testing. On the cap, a power bank replaces the laptop, the MB stays at home and the board runs by itself. CapSight supplements the white cane; it does not replace it.
